What does "keeping receipts" mean?

By Built by ArgoPublished 29 October 20265 min read

Short answer

In slang, receipts are proof: screenshots, messages, emails, photos, dates. Keeping receipts means saving that evidence so you can show what someone really said or did if they deny it later. "Show me the receipts" means "prove it."

Like a shop receipt proves you bought something, a screenshot proves a conversation happened. The metaphor is that simple. The phrase is used in arguments, celebrity feuds, workplace disputes and group chats, usually when someone's version of events is about to be contradicted.

Where "receipts" came from

Nobody knows exactly who first used "receipts" this way. Dictionary.com's slang dictionary says it appears to have started in Black English before spreading into mainstream use. A few moments stand out:

  • 2002: In an ABC interview with Diane Sawyer, Whitney Houston denied tabloid claims about her drug use and said: "I want to see the receipts." It became one of the best-known early uses.
  • Late 2000s and 2010s: "Show me the receipts" spread online, both as a reference to the Houston interview and as a general demand for proof.
  • 2016: During the dispute between Kanye West and Taylor Swift, Kim Kardashian posted video of a phone call between the two. Media coverage called it "receipts", and the word went fully mainstream.

How people use it

  • "I keep receipts." I save evidence, so don't try to rewrite history with me.
  • "Show me the receipts." Prove it.
  • "She came with receipts." She backed up her claims with evidence.
  • "The receipts are in." The proof has been posted.

The tone ranges from joking to deadly serious. Among friends it's usually the former. In a workplace dispute it's sensible practice.

How to keep receipts properly

If you're going to keep receipts, keep ones that hold up. A screenshot without a date proves less than you'd hope.

  1. Capture the context. Make sure the screenshot shows who said it, the date and time, and the messages around it. A single line cropped on its own is easy to dispute.
  2. Write down when it happened. Memory drifts. "Last spring" becomes "a couple of months ago" surprisingly fast. Note the date while you still know it.
  3. Keep the original where you can. The original message or email carries more weight than a picture of it. Don't delete the thread.
  4. Store it somewhere you control. Screenshots get lost in camera rolls among thousands of photos. Put them somewhere organised and back them up.
  5. Think before you share. Posting someone's private messages can create a bigger problem than the one you're documenting. For anything with legal weight, such as harassment or an employment dispute, ask a professional how to preserve evidence. Most receipts never need to leave your phone.
